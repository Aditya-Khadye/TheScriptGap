# Formula-weight sensitivity: Script Servedness Score

Generated by `analysis/formula_sweep.py` (step 0.05, 231 weight points) from committed data (v1.1 diversity). The baseline was verified against `data/final/script_servedness.csv` before sweeping.

## Headline

- **Support vs diversity trade-off: robust.** With demand left out, both claims hold for any support share from **30% to 75%** (rest on diversity), and the Indic four stay the bottom four from **0% to 75%**. An even 50/50 split gives Spearman 0.979 vs the SSS (order: Tamil < Bengali < Devanagari < Telugu < Arabic < Han < Katakana < Cyrillic < Latin).
- **Demand in the shipped formula is inert.** log10(exposure) varies only 1.3% across the nine scripts, so raising the denominator to a power changes nothing until gamma = **9**, and the first change (Latin and Katakana) is a swap inside one tier. The shipped SSS is, in practice, a supply-side score (support x diversity).
- **Giving demand real weight breaks the conclusions.** Once inputs are standardized, both claims survive a majority of support/diversity splits only up to a demand weight of about **15%**. The Indic-four claim dies at 45% and the well-served three at 30%.

## Part A: demand exponent in the shipped formula

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| log10(exposure) range | 7.243 to 7.338 |
| Spread of the demand term | 1.32% |
| Raw demand max/min | 1.25x |
| Raw support max/min (for scale) | 175x |
| Smallest gamma that reorders any pair | 9 |

Raw demand itself only spans 1.25x across scripts, consistent with the bundler-font confound documented in `exposure_research/DEMAND_PROVENANCE.md`. Any transform that stretches it enough to matter (min-max, z-score) is amplifying a mostly-confounded signal.

## What this means for the paper

1. The robustness claim that is defensible: the tiering is stable across a wide range of support/diversity trade-offs. That is the sentence to write.
2. The README's "real font choice per unit of web demand" framing overstates what the shipped score does. Either drop demand from the score and report it as a separate axis (as complexity already is), or keep it and say plainly that its numerical effect on the ranking is nil.
3. Demand cannot be given meaningful weight until the `subset=` upgrade lands. At current quality, any weighting that lets it matter mostly re-sorts scripts by a bundler-font artifact.
4. Han's position is the least stable result in the set. It depends on whether you reward its high visual variety or penalize its low family count, and it has the lowest measured demand. With Arabic it sits at the 0.3 tier boundary in the SSS (see `analysis/v11_checks.py`).

## Method notes

- Inputs: Google Fonts family counts, ViT-B/16 mean cosine distance (raw, not min-max), HTTP Archive font-request volume; all log10 then z-scored (population sd).
- Because ranks are invariant to rescaling all weights together, the simplex covers every non-negative weighting up to scale.
- This tests the formula, not measurement error. For input noise see `analysis/v11_checks.py` and `analysis/sensitivity.py` (one-at-a-time shocks).
