# Formula-weight sensitivity: Script Servedness Score

Generated by `analysis/formula_sweep.py` (step 0.05, 231 weight points) from committed v1.0 data. The baseline was verified against `data/final/script_servedness.csv` before sweeping.

## Headline

- **Support vs diversity trade-off: robust.** With demand left out, every v1.0 claim holds for any support share from **15% to 75%** (rest on diversity). An even 50/50 split gives the identical v1.0 ordering (Spearman 0.996; below 1 only because v1.0 ties Tamil and Bengali at zero).
- **Demand in the shipped formula is inert.** log10(exposure) varies only 1.3% across the nine scripts, so raising the denominator to a power changes nothing until gamma = **27**. v1.0 is, in practice, a supply-side score (support x diversity).
- **Giving demand real weight breaks the conclusions.** Once inputs are standardized, all three claims survive a majority of support/diversity splits only up to a demand weight of about **15%**. The six-script Underserved set never survives past 30%, the Indic-four claim dies at 45%, and Latin stops being best served at 50%.

## Part A: demand exponent in the shipped formula

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| log10(exposure) range | 7.243 to 7.338 |
| Spread of the demand term | 1.32% |
| Raw demand max/min | 1.25x |
| Raw support max/min (for scale) | 175x |
| Smallest gamma that reorders any pair | 27 |

Raw demand itself only spans 1.25x across scripts, consistent with the bundler-font confound documented in `exposure_research/DEMAND_PROVENANCE.md`. Any transform that stretches it enough to matter (min-max, z-score) is amplifying a mostly-confounded signal.

## What this means for the paper

1. The robustness claim that is defensible: the tiering is stable across a wide range of support/diversity trade-offs. That is the sentence to write.
2. The README's "real font choice per unit of web demand" framing overstates what the shipped score does. Either drop demand from the score and report it as a separate axis (as complexity already is), or keep it and say plainly that its numerical effect on the ranking is nil.
3. Demand cannot be given meaningful weight until the `subset=` upgrade lands. At current quality, any weighting that lets it matter mostly re-sorts scripts by a bundler-font artifact.
4. Han's tier is the least stable result in the set. It depends on whether you reward its high visual variety or penalize its low family count, and it has the lowest measured demand.

## Method notes

- Inputs: Google Fonts family counts, ViT-B/16 mean cosine distance (raw, not min-max), HTTP Archive font-request volume; all log10 then z-scored (population sd).
- Because ranks are invariant to rescaling all weights together, the simplex covers every non-negative weighting up to scale.
- This tests the formula, not measurement error. For input noise see `analysis/sensitivity.py` (one-at-a-time shocks) on the Sensitivity-Analysis branch.
